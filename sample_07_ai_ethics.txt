For this assignment on the ethical implications of automated decision-making systems, I focused on the use of AI in loan approval processes as a case study. One of the main concerns raised in the readings was that a model trained on historical lending data can end up reproducing past biases, even if the sensitive attribute like race or gender is explicitly removed from the training data, because other correlated features can act as proxies. This made me question whether removing an attribute is actually enough to make a system fair, or whether fairness needs to be tested directly on the model's outputs across different groups. I also found it interesting that different definitions of fairness, such as equal false positive rates versus equal approval rates, can actually conflict with each other mathematically, meaning a system cannot always satisfy every fairness criterion at once.
