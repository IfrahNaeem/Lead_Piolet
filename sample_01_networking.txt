Computer networks are usually explained through layered models because a single network has to handle many different jobs at once, from moving raw bits across a cable to making sure a webpage loads correctly in a browser. The OSI model splits this into seven layers, but in practice most real systems follow the simpler four-layer TCP/IP model. What I found most confusing at first was the difference between the transport layer and the network layer, since both seem to deal with getting data from one place to another. The clearest way I found to understand it is that the network layer only cares about getting a packet to the right machine, while the transport layer cares about getting it to the right application on that machine, and about whether the data arrives complete and in order.
