The statistics assignment this week asked us to interpret a p-value from a hypothesis test comparing two teaching methods, and I initially misunderstood what the number actually represented. I assumed a p-value of 0.03 meant there was a 3 percent chance the null hypothesis was true, but after reviewing the lecture notes again, I realized it actually represents the probability of observing a result this extreme, assuming the null hypothesis is true in the first place. This distinction matters a lot for how the result should be reported, since a small p-value tells us the observed difference is unlikely under the null hypothesis, not that one teaching method is definitely better. I also had to be careful not to confuse statistical significance with practical significance, since a very large sample size can produce a small p-value even when the actual difference between groups is small.
