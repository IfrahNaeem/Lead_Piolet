Normalization in database design is something that sounds more complicated in textbooks than it actually is once you work through an example. The main goal is just to avoid storing the same piece of information in more than one place, because that is what eventually causes update anomalies. In our assignment, the original table listed a student's name and department next to every course they were enrolled in, which meant the department name was repeated for every single course row. After splitting this into a separate students table and a separate enrollments table, connected using a student ID, updating a student's department only required changing one row instead of many. This made the practical benefit of second and third normal form much clearer to me than the formal definitions did.
