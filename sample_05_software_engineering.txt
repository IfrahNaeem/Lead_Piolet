For our software engineering assignment, we were asked to compare the waterfall model with an agile approach using a hypothetical university management system as the example project. The waterfall model works well on paper because every phase is clearly defined and documented before moving to the next one, which makes it easier to track progress against a fixed plan. However, in our case study the requirements kept changing after the client saw an early prototype, which is exactly the situation waterfall struggles with since going back to an earlier phase is costly. An agile approach with short sprints would have allowed the requirements to be adjusted after each demo without restarting the whole planning phase. This assignment made the common criticism of waterfall, that it assumes requirements never change, feel much more concrete than it did when we first read about it.
